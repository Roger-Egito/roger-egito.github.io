### The project

<Media image={lobby} alt="Dim hotel lobby with a ceiling lamp, wood paneling and a camcorder timestamp reading Jul 7 1997, 00:00" caption="The hotel at the start of the loop">

Hotel 77 is a first-person narrative horror game from Ludomancer Studio, set in a haunted hotel caught in a time loop. Players walk its shifting corridors, spot what is wrong, and have to make the right choice to move time forward.

The team had about ten people, and the game is localized into ten languages with full English audio. I worked on it in two stretches: the hotel's level design in December 2024, and the bedrooms and a new set of anomalies in December 2025.

</Media>

<Work section="designer">

<Media image={corridor} alt="Long hotel corridor lined with doors, wall lamps, shelves of vases and an armchair" caption="One of the hotel's corridors" side="right">

**Rooms.** In the first pass I built and polished several of the hotel's rooms, including the Outlast room, the skull room and the dark room, and added locked doors, the carpets and the letter. My level design branch was merged in January 2025.

</Media>

<Media image={bedroom} alt="A hotel bedroom with green striped wallpaper, a made bed, a bedside lamp, an armchair and a curtained window" caption="The bedroom I designed, the same behind all 8 doors">

**Bedrooms.** In the second pass the hotel needed bedrooms. Each hallway has 8 doors, and in a game about spotting what's wrong, 8 different rooms would mean 8 rooms to memorize and 8 doors to check every loop.

So I designed one bedroom and put it behind all 8 doors, which leaves players a single room to learn. Only one door in the hallway is unlocked, so they never check all 8 either. The unlocked door is random, except when the anomaly is in a bedroom: then that bedroom always spawns behind the same door.

</Media>

<Media image={getOut} alt='Hallway with "GET OUT" smeared across the wall above a door, between two statues' caption="GET OUT, one of my hallway anomalies" side="right">

**Anomalies.** I created 17 anomaly variants and 4 themed room variants across both passes. In the hallways they include blood handprints around the fire alarm, luggage turned into coffins, a dead ghost sitting at the start of the hallway, GET OUT written across the wall, statues that turn to watch you, a skull inside a diver helmet, and Nevermore, where the same helmet hides a bloody raven. In the bedrooms they include a bloody bathtub, an evil TV, a hole in the wardrobe, a lamp that is simply wrong, and a crying zombie.

</Media>

<Media image={evilTv} alt="An old television on a bedroom dresser, its screen filled by a giant staring eye" caption="The evil TV, one of my bedroom anomalies">

To me, a good anomaly rewards paying attention. It doesn't try to jumpscare you hard. You get a little scared when you realize what's wrong with the room. Some of mine are obvious and scary on purpose, and some are really hard to spot. I always added a black substance near the anomaly, to make it a tiny bit easier to see and to give all of them a shared motif. When an anomaly is a jumpscare, it should feel unique. In my Outlast room, the power goes out partway through and your night vision switches on by itself.

</Media>

<Media image={shadowMan} alt="A dark hotel room seen past stacked luggage, with a tall shadowy figure silhouetted against the only lit wall at the far end" caption="The shadow man, from the trailer" side="right">

My favorite is the shadow man, in my dark room. The lights are off and a huge shadow stands at the far end, looking at you. It gives an immediate "oh no" feeling without being a jumpscare. Walk toward it, and the lights come back on and it's gone.

**Result.** Every anomaly went through review, and two of mine were cut. A shadow hiding behind the luggage, given away only by its breathing, was too hard to spot. A door left ajar with a baby crying behind it overlapped with another developer's crying baby.

</Media>

</Work>

<Work section="developer">

<Media image={redHallway} alt="The same hotel hallway lit entirely in red" caption="Every loop can change the hotel">

I wrote eight scripts in Unity and C# to bring the anomalies to life: objects and heads that turn to face the player, triggers that fire when the player gets close, props that vanish on approach or after a timer, a random door unlocker, a rotation reset trigger, and a manager for the lamp anomaly.

I also helped fix bugs across the game.

</Media>

</Work>

<Work section="writer">

The hotel needed a past that players could piece together, so I created its lore and wrote the letter and the audio logs that tell its backstory. I also added 18 new entries for my dialogue to the game's localization tables.

</Work>

<Work section="other">

**Trailers.** I cut trailers for the game.

</Work>
